## What is Noorani Qaida?

*Qaida* means "a foundation" or "a set of rules". Noorani Qaida is a widely used beginner's book, named after its compiler, Shaykh Noor Muhammad Haqqani. It is used by children and adults around the world as their very first step towards reading the Qur'an.

Its strength is its order: each lesson adds just one new idea, and every lesson is practised before moving on. By the end, the student has met every sound and sign they need to open the Mushaf and begin reading.

## What does it teach, step by step?

A typical Noorani Qaida course moves through stages like these:

1. **The Arabic letters** — their names, shapes and correct pronunciation.
2. **Joined letters** — how each letter changes shape at the start, middle and end of a word.
3. **Short vowels (harakat)** — fatha, kasra and damma.
4. **Tanween** — the double vowel marks that add an "n" sound.
5. **The long vowels (madd letters)** — alif, waw and ya.
6. **Sukoon and shaddah** — stopping on a letter, and doubling it.
7. **Reading practice** — words, then short phrases taken from the Qur'an.

Basic Tajweed is introduced along the way, so good habits are formed from the very first lesson.

## How long does it take?

It depends on the student's age, how often they have class, and how much they practise between lessons. Young children usually need more time and shorter lessons; adults often move faster. With regular one-to-one classes, the teacher can move at exactly the student's pace — slowing down where needed and moving on when a lesson is mastered.

## Why start with Noorani Qaida?

Skipping the foundation is the most common reason people struggle with reading the Qur'an later. Mistakes in individual letters — especially letters that sound similar — are much harder to fix once they become habits. Noorani Qaida gets the foundation right from the start.

## What comes next?

After Noorani Qaida, students usually move on to **Tajweed** and **Qur'an reading**, building fluency page by page — and, for those who wish, on to **Hifz**.
