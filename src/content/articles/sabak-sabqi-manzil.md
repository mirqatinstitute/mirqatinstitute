## Why revision matters so much

Memorising a page once is not enough — without regular revision, memorisation fades. Every experienced Hifz teacher will tell you that **revision is half of Hifz**. The sabak–sabqi–manzil system is a simple, proven way to make sure every part of what a student has memorised is revised regularly.

## The three parts

### Sabak — the new lesson

The *sabak* is the new portion the student memorises for the day — a few lines, half a page or a page, depending on the student. It is recited to the teacher, who checks every word and corrects Tajweed and pronunciation before the student moves on.

### Sabqi — recent revision

The *sabqi* is revision of what was memorised recently — for example, the last several pages or the current juz. Recent memorisation is the easiest to lose, so it is revised every day until it is firm.

### Manzil — older revision

The *manzil* is revision of everything memorised earlier. The older portions are divided into daily amounts and cycled through, so that the whole of what the student has memorised is revised regularly — for example, a student who has memorised ten juz might revise one or two juz a day as manzil.

## How the balance changes over time

At the start of Hifz, the sabak takes up most of the time. As the amount memorised grows, the manzil grows too, and more of each day goes to revision. This is normal and healthy — it is what keeps the whole Qur'an strong.

## Tips for strong revision

- **Never skip revision** to make room for more new lessons.
- **Recite revision to someone** — a teacher or parent — not just silently.
- **Mark weak pages** and give them extra attention.
- **Keep the same Mushaf**, so the layout of each page becomes familiar.
- **After completing Hifz, keep a manzil routine for life** — this is what keeps the Qur'an with you.
