## Signs your child is ready

Age is only a rough guide. These signs matter more:

- They can **sit and focus** for a short lesson.
- They can **follow simple instructions** and repeat sounds after a teacher.
- They **recognise shapes** and are beginning to learn letters in any language.
- They are **curious** and enjoy listening to the Qur'an at home.

If your child shows most of these, they are probably ready to begin.

## What lessons look like for young children

For young beginners, lessons start with **Noorani Qaida** — learning the Arabic letters and their sounds one by one. Good early lessons are:

- **Short and focused**, so the child finishes before losing attention.
- **Regular**, because little and often works better than long, infrequent sessions.
- **Encouraging**, with plenty of praise for effort.
- **One-to-one**, so the teacher can hear and gently correct each sound.

## Tips for parents

1. **Keep a routine.** The same time each day helps learning become a habit.
2. **Practise a little at home.** Five minutes of revision between classes makes a big difference.
3. **Listen together.** Playing recitation at home helps children absorb the sounds.
4. **Praise effort, not speed.** Every child moves at their own pace.
5. **Stay involved.** Sit in on a class now and then, and talk to the teacher about progress.

## It is never too late

Many people begin learning the Qur'an as teenagers or adults — including new Muslims and parents learning alongside their children. Adults often progress quickly because they can practise independently. The first step is the same for everyone: a strong foundation with Noorani Qaida.
